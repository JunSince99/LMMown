What the percentage means

If a 100 mm reference prints as 95 mm at a 100% setting, the calculated correction is 100 × 100 ÷ 95 = 105.263%. That is a starting point for another proof, not a guarantee. Different apps, paper settings and drivers can change the result.

When one percentage will not work

If the horizontal and vertical measurements differ, changing one percentage scales both directions together. Remeasure first. Then check paper size, borderless enlargement and printer settings. A correction made in one workflow should be verified again when that workflow changes.

This tool treats a difference above 0.5 mm between the two 100 mm measurements as uneven. That is a practical diagnostic threshold, not a manufacturer's specification or a promise of sewing-pattern accuracy.

Common reasons prints come out small

  • Fit / Shrink: the app reduces the page to fit inside printable margins.
  • Paper mismatch: the document is A4 while the tray or driver is set to Letter, or the reverse.
  • Borderless settings: some drivers enlarge the image to reach the paper edge.
  • Image resolution: changing DPI metadata does not add detail or certify a physical size.

For a sewing pattern, use its own test square as the final check. For artwork, check the image dimensions and crop as well as the printer. Do not cut fabric or print a full batch based only on this calculator.

Method & sources

The PDF uses 72 points per inch and 25.4 mm per inch. Its square is 100 × 100 mm in PDF coordinates. Software checks cover the dimensions and arithmetic. A physical printer test has not been performed.
